## Follow-up to the meta-review: completed corrections and persistence baseline

We agree that the completed work does not establish cross-geometry generalization or replace the broader learned-surrogate comparisons requested in the reviews. We report the following update because it directly addresses two concerns identified in the meta-review: the absence of a standard forecasting baseline and ambiguity about the autoregressive evaluation.

After rerunning the pipeline under the revised exclusion protocol, including retraining the shared autoencoder, we evaluated the resulting autoregressive checkpoint against persistence on all 165 available 40-frame validation sequences. Each sequence provides 12 context frames (100 ms), after which the model generates 28 forecast frames (233.3 ms); the agreed U* = 10 exclusion remains preserved. The model has lower centroid-velocity RMSE than persistence at every reported horizon: 4.81 × 10⁻⁴ versus 5.40 × 10⁻⁴ at 8.3 ms, 1.188 × 10⁻³ versus 1.593 × 10⁻³ at 100 ms, and 1.534 × 10⁻³ versus 2.343 × 10⁻³ at 233.3 ms. Final-horizon improvement is 34.6%, the 95% bootstrap interval for persistence RMSE minus model RMSE is [6.80 × 10⁻⁴, 9.40 × 10⁻⁴], and 78.2% of sequences individually favor the model. Positive improvements also hold separately for wake-targeted and random-location sequences, all three velocity components, MAE, and mean vector L2. This rules out a trivial persistence or frozen-field explanation for the evaluated rollouts, without constituting a formal stationarity test.

The response also discloses and corrects the stage-membership error in Sections 3.3 and 3.5, replaces the erroneous machine-precision enstrophy claim with the corrected cross-source evaluation, and clarifies that production reconstruction is strictly centroid-only without overlap averaging. We have correspondingly narrowed the contribution to a use-inspired, system-level finding about local nonlinear representation learning on experimental volumetric flow data rather than a new Transformer primitive. We do not present these corrections as resolving the remaining requests for FNO, Transolver, ConvLSTM-style, or cross-geometry evaluations; they convert the persistence baseline from planned to completed, remove several evaluation ambiguities, and delimit the claims more precisely.
